# Candidate evidence review — 60 target-zone candidates

This review asks whether the target zone contains a useful, inexpensive,
non-approved cancer candidate after checking the graph route, direction, human
testing, results, and primary literature. It is a targeted evidence pass, not a
formal systematic review and not a precision estimate.

## Current status (2026-08-01)

All 60 rows have been reviewed. The working sheet is
`CANDIDATE_REVIEW_60.csv`; the human annotations are kept separately in
`CANDIDATE_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.csv` so regenerating graph metadata cannot erase the
review.

The review changed several assumptions that were present in the original
packet:

- The deliberately invalid cases are six Li-Fraumeni rows and four EGFR-GBM
  rows, not the reverse.
- The four EGFR-GBM agents do not share one evidentiary state. Dacomitinib has a
  published negative phase II result, amivantamab is in a recruiting trial, and
  no pair-specific result was found for brigatinib or lazertinib.
- `already tried` is not a scientific outcome. Cimetidine-RCC produced rare
  durable responses, while suramin-RCC was inactive in two published studies.
- `not approved` is not evidence of failure, especially for inexpensive
  generics.
- `no direct evidence found` is unresolved standing, never a pass.

The evidence-state counts are descriptive: 8 tested signals, 4 mixed tested
results, 2 published inactive results, 4 active trials without results, 4
preclinical-only pairs, 17 pairs with no direct evidence found, and the
remainder split among invalid routes, unresolved records, and other
non-comparable states. Do not turn these into a precision figure.

## Graph receipts were checked separately

`terminal_tier`, `terminal_provenance`, and
`terminal_receipt_assessment` are regenerated from the active `tier1.db` by:

```powershell
python -m validation.enrich_candidate_review `
  --out reports\candidate_review_2026-08-01\CANDIDATE_REVIEW_60.csv
```

The 60 rows use 21 unique PubMed identifiers on terminal edges. Their NCBI
existence check is in `TERMINAL_PMID_AUDIT.csv`; the human relevance review is
in `TERMINAL_PMID_REVIEW.csv`.

One identifier, PMID 23177518 on KDR→GIST (rows R42 and R44), does not resolve.
Several others resolve but are contextual or incidental rather than adequate
support for the tier assigned. A valid candidate in the literature does not
retroactively make a broken graph receipt valid: cabozantinib-GIST is the clear
example.

The existence audit can be rerun with:

```powershell
python -m validation.audit_terminal_pmids `
  --out reports\candidate_review_2026-08-01\TERMINAL_PMID_AUDIT.csv
```

NCBI can verify that an identifier resolves. It cannot decide whether the paper
supports the edge; that is why the relevance review is a separate artifact.

`TRIAL_EVIDENCE_60.json` is a reproducible registry inventory generated by
`validation/fetch_negative_trials.py`. It distinguishes active studies,
posted results, linked publications, aged completed records without posted
results, and operational stops. It deliberately leaves result polarity for
human review and flags multi-arm co-occurrence where the drug-disease pairing
is not established by the record.

## Evidence fields — not a one-word verdict

The old `WORTH_READING / ALREADY_TRIED / WRONG / UNCLEAR` column was removed.
It collapsed evidence access, study status, outcome, and candidate quality into
one label. The replacement keeps those dimensions separate:

| column | question answered |
|---|---|
| `evidence_state` | What kind of direct evidence was found? |
| `human_testing_status` | Was the pair untested, active, completed, terminated, or only indirectly represented? |
| `result_signal` | Was the observed signal positive, negative, mixed, unavailable, or not tested? |
| `candidate_assessment` | What should happen to this candidate in this pipeline? |
| `negative_evidence_found` | Was actual negative evidence found? `NO` does not mean supported. |
| `what_kind_of_negative_evidence` | What exactly was negative, absent, operationally stopped, or unresolved? |
| `evidence_sources` | Primary paper or registry receipts opened during review. |

Evidence states are intentionally more specific than pass/fail. Important
examples include `TESTED_SIGNAL`, `TESTED_INACTIVE_PUBLISHED`, `TESTED_MIXED`,
`ACTIVE_TRIAL_NO_RESULT`, `PRECLINICAL_ONLY`, and
`NO_DIRECT_EVIDENCE_FOUND`.

## Bottom line

The target zone contains plausible inexpensive candidates, but this pass did
not confirm a novel discovery produced by the graph.

The strongest cheap-drug row is atorvastatin→pancreatic cancer: a 2025
single-arm phase II study reported an encouraging human signal, but without a
comparator it does not establish efficacy, and the idea was already in clinical
testing. Propranolol→RCC and mebendazole→HCC remain interesting but
inconclusive; their graph routes are also weaker than their literature stories.

The honest result is therefore: the graph retrieves real mechanistic and
clinically active relationships, but no row in this sample is yet both novel
and confirmed useful. That is a useful boundary, not a failed experiment.

## Known pipeline defects exposed by this review

- R05 uses a drug (`Ruxolitinib`) as the horn intermediate.
- Li-Fraumeni syndrome is represented as if it were a tumour indication.
- Some disease-level candidates require a mutation that the horn does not
  encode, such as KRAS G12C for adagrasib or sotorasib.
- Terminal tiers range from 24 `ESTABLISHED` to 28 `HYPOTHESIS` and 8
  `SPECULATIVE`; hiding this distinction materially changed how rows looked.
- Trial search returns false positives unless the named intervention is checked
  against the intervention module.
- Completed trials need result/publication review; `whyStopped` alone cannot
  detect efficacy failure.

Rows remain in their original interleaved order so the review sample is auditable.

## Application materialization

The reviewed CSV/JSON artifacts remain the source records. `python -m
evidence.build` materializes them into `data/evidence/evidence.db` for read-only
Pair-detail queries and local FTS5 search. The evidence database is separate
from `tier1.db`; building it cannot alter graph scoring or benchmark results.
